I am a first-year Ph.D. student in the [Department of Statistics](https://lsa.umich.edu/stats) at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor. Before coming to Michigan, I completed my B.S. in Statistics at the [University of Science and Technology of China (USTC)](http://en.ustc.edu.cn/).

My research interests center on **statistical network analysis**, and I also work on **synthetic data generation for time series** and **model selection for latent class models**. Some of the questions I'm currently exploring:

* Generating realistic synthetic time series data using approaches such as reverse Markov learning and engression, building on earlier work with LSTM encoder-decoder architectures and variational autoencoders.
* BIC-based model selection for latent class models, using Monte Carlo simulation studies to understand the computational and statistical trade-offs involved.
* Statistical methodology for analyzing network-structured data.

Alongside research, I work as a teaching assistant for undergraduate statistics courses at Michigan, where I help design lesson materials and interactive activities — see the [Teaching](/teaching/) page for more.

Feel free to reach out if you'd like to chat about research, teaching, or anything statistics-related.

A bit more about me
======
I did my undergraduate degree in Statistics at USTC, where I first got interested in the mathematical foundations of statistics — probability theory, statistical inference, and modeling — before shifting toward the more applied and computational questions I work on now.

This site is very much a work in progress as I get settled into the PhD — check back for updates on publications, talks, and projects as they happen.
